# UK Railway Sales Performance Analysis

![](https://github.com/hamadkamorudeen/UK-Railway-Sales-Performance-Analysis/blob/main/Train.jpg)



## Introduction
This project analyzes over 31,000 railway ticket transactions to understand how revenue flows across stations, ticket types, customer segments, and time periods. The goal was to take raw transactional records and turn them into a clean, interactive Excel dashboard that gives management a clear picture of what is working and what needs attention.


The analysis covers three core questions: what is happening across the network, where disruptions and revenue gaps exist, and who is driving the most value.


## Business Objectives
The project was built to answer:

- How is total ticket revenue distributed across stations and routes?
- Which ticket types and classes generate the most revenue?
- How do customer attributes like railcard ownership influence spending?
- Which time periods carry the highest journey volume and disruption risk?
- What is the relationship between delays, cancellations, and refund requests?
- Where should management focus to protect revenue and improve reliability?
  


## Data Description
One dataset was provided containing 31,653 transaction level records across 18 columns:
- Transaction ID: Unique identifier per ticket purchase
- Date of Purchase: When the ticket was bought
- Purchase Type: Online or Station
- Payment Method: Credit Card, Contactless, Debit Card
- Railcard: Discount card type or None
- Ticket Class: Standard or First Class
- Ticket Type: Advance, Off-Peak, or Anytime
- Price: Final ticket cost in GBP
- Departure Station: Origin station
- Arrival Destination: Destination station
- Date of Journey: Actual travel date
- Journey Status: On Time, Delayed, or Cancelled
- Reason for Delay: Cause of disruption if applicable
- Refund Request: Whether the customer requested a refund

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- PivotTables and PivotCharts
- VLOOKUP, AVERAGEIF, COUNTIF, IF, HOUR, TEXT functions
- Manual data validation and cleaning
- Excel Tables for dynamic referencing


## Data Preparation Process
The analysis followed a structured cleaning and preparation workflow:

- Raw dataset imported into Excel without modification
- Missing values identified across three columns and resolved with documented assumptions
- Inconsistent text entries standardized across the Reason for Delay column
- Helper columns created for Year, Month, Day of Week, and Time of Day
- A Railcard comparison table built manually for segment level pricing analysis
- Clean analysis table converted into an Excel Table for consistent pivot table behavior



![](https://github.com/hamadkamorudeen/UK-Railway-Sales-Performance-Analysis/blob/main/Clean%20Dataset.png)



### Cleaning assumptions documented:

- Blank Railcard entries filled with "None" representing customers with no discount card
- Blank Reason for Delay filled with "No Delay" for on-time journeys
- Blank Actual Arrival Time filled with "Cancelled" matching the journey status
- "Signal failure" standardized to "Signal Failure"
- "Weather Conditions" standardized to "Weather"
- "Staff Shortage" standardized to "Staffing"



## Analysis Approach
All analysis was performed using Excel PivotTables.

Key techniques applied:

- Revenue aggregation across stations, routes, ticket types, and time periods
- Customer count and transaction count using COUNTA and COUNT functions
- Time based segmentation using YEAR and HOUR helper columns
- Railcard segment pricing comparison using AVERAGE formula across grouped categories
- Cross tabulation of refund requests against journey status
- Revenue by day of week to identify high and low performing periods

## Key Findings

- Total revenue across the dataset is £741,921 from 31,653 transactions
- 86.8 percent of journeys ran on time, while 7.2 percent were delayed and 5.9 percent cancelled
- Weather and Signal Failure are the two largest causes of delay by volume
- Staffing related delays (809 incidents) are the only major controllable delay cause
- Customers without a railcard pay an average of £27.43 per ticket versus £15.10 for railcard holders, a 45 percent gap
- Cancelled journeys result in a refund request 30 percent of the time
- Delayed journeys result in a refund request 24 percent of the time
- On-time journeys produce zero refund requests
- Morning is the highest volume travel period with 10,984 journeys
- Wednesday generates the most revenue at £111,577 across the week
- Credit Card accounts for 60.5 percent of all transactions
- Advance tickets are the most purchased type at 55.5 percent of all sales


## Business Implications

- Every delay or cancellation creates a 24 to 30 percent chance of a refund request, meaning operational reliability is directly tied to revenue protection
- Staffing is the only major delay cause within the company's control and represents the clearest opportunity for quick improvement
- Railcard holders are price sensitive customers who travel regularly and could be engaged with targeted loyalty or upsell campaigns
- Revenue is heavily concentrated in morning weekday travel, making that window both the highest opportunity and highest risk period
- A small number of departure stations drive the majority of journey volume, meaning infrastructure and staffing investment in those locations has outsized impact
